01 / CONTEXT

A shared marketplace. Different operations.

How can seller groups guide assortment, logistics and support investigation? The public source contains 99,441 orders and 3,095 sellers. This study focuses on the 592 sellers with at least 10 delivered orders in the development cohort.

Eligible sellers do not represent the entire low-volume tail. Groups are not quality ratings and do not determine incentives or penalties.

02 / METHOD

Profiles use only information available at the cutoff.

Seven dimensions combine volume, order value, freight share, late delivery, reviews, interstate reach and category concentration. Reviews and late delivery use single-seller orders to avoid ambiguous attribution.

04 / EVIDENCE

Purchase patterns explain the main differences.

Higher-ticket sellers have greater category concentration and a lower relative freight burden. Delivery delays and reviews were similar across groups; these data do not support labelling sellers as “good” or “bad”.

05 / MODEL COMPARISON

Two groups were enough for this version.

We compared 15 K-Means, Gaussian Mixture and Ward configurations, from 2 to 6 groups. Two-group K-Means had the highest silhouette among candidates meeting group-size and resampling-stability criteria.

A silhouette of 0.166 indicates overlap: the groups simplify hypothesis exploration without proving natural classes. Mean ARI of 0.871 comes from 12 subsamples of 80%; this is not a confidence interval.

06 / TEMPORAL STABILITY

A seller profile can change over time.

Among 372 sellers eligible in both windows, 86.6% kept their group. Temporal ARI of 0.533 is lower than resampling stability. Product mix, seasonality and window duration also contribute to migration.

07 / NEXT STEPS

Turn seller profiles into testable hypotheses.

  1. Investigate assortment, availability and specialist support for higher-ticket sellers.
  2. Assess baskets, logistics consolidation and freight burden for lower-ticket sellers.
  3. Repeat the analysis with recent data and fixed-duration windows.
  4. Test interventions with controlled experiments, actual costs and monitoring by region/category.
