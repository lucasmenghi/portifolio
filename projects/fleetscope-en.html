FLEETSCOPE / SCANIA CASE / DECISION TREE

Diagnose failures, expose decisions

A single decision tree to classify air-pressure-system failures in Scania trucks, support technical triage and expose the cost of each error type.

Explore resultsView project

01 / BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING

Triage with different error costs

When a team investigates failure records, missing an APS issue can carry more weight than a false alert. The dataset distinguishes failure of a specific APS component from failures in other components. Negative cases are not healthy trucks. This case concerns historical diagnostics, without a future-failure forecast horizon.

Challenge criterion: false positives cost 10 units; false negatives, 500. These weights are not currency or an estimate of real operating costs.

02 / DATA UNDERSTANDING & PREPARATION

Real data and availability limits

60,000 official training records, including 1,000 positives; 16,000 official test records, including 375 positives. There are 170 anonymous attributes. Missing values use training-only medians and indicators. No vehicle identifiers or global calendar are supplied: vehicle-disjoint or temporal validation cannot be established. Operational availability of inputs has not been verified either.

03 / MODELING

Single tree, selection and freezing

Six single-tree candidates comparing depth, complexity pruning and minimum leaf size. Selected by development AP: tree_d12, depth 12 and 23 leaves. Official training split: 36,000 fit, 9,000 development, 9,000 sigmoid calibration and 6,000 policy rows. Threshold minimizes FP×10 + FN×500 on policy data; everything is frozen before official testing.

How the tree decides

The plot displays only the first four tree levels. Full rules are available in the repository. Names such as aa_000 are anonymized, without published physical meaning: thresholds are not described as pressure or wear limits. Leaf proportions are raw training proportions; the app presents calibrated probabilities. Impurity importance is not a causal effect.

04 / EVALUATION

Results on the held-out official test

Logistic regression had better ranking (AP 0.818) and lower Brier. The tree identified 356 of 375 APS failures, missed 19 and produced 768 false alerts. With frozen policies, its challenge cost was 33.6% lower than logistic regression. This does not establish general algorithm superiority or realized savings.

06 / LIMITATIONS & NEXT STEPS

Limits and next step

One split and one seed sequence, without confidence intervals. Vehicles may repeat across records and no IDs allow an audit. Prevalence differs between training and testing. Scenario score ties are broken by source order, which can change queue composition. No demonstrated operational efficacy or savings. Before a pilot, validate availability, vehicle independence, stability, calibration, real costs and impacts with specialists.
